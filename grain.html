GRAIN investigates how ordinary grains self-organize under vibration. Our central focus is granular convection: the circulation that can drive segregation effects such as the Brazil nut effect, where larger particles rise to the surface of a shaken mixture.

What We Study

Sand can act like a solid, flow like a liquid, and still be neither one.

Granular materials are collections of macroscopic particles such as sand, seeds, glass beads, and powders. They are large enough that gravity, not thermal energy, governs their motion. Grains do not move on their own; they move when they are pushed.

That is why granular matter is so strange. It can support weight like a solid, pour like a liquid, dissipate energy in every collision, and suddenly jam mid-flow. Under vibration, those local collisions can organize into global convection rolls that move grains through the entire bed.

No Equilibrium

Every collision dissipates energy as heat and sound, so the system must be continually driven.

Force Chains

Stress travels through filamentary contact networks rather than distributing uniformly like a fluid.

Brazil Nut Effect

Large grains can rise to the top through a balance of void filling, inertia, convection, and pressure gradients.

Convection

Wall friction and vibration can generate counter-rotating circulation rolls across the whole bed.

Research Program at Catawba College

GRAIN uses controlled experiments and quantitative analysis to ask how particle properties, forcing direction, environment, and container design shape the onset and dynamics of granular convection.

Segregation Onset

Determine how grain-property ratios and fill depth control when segregation begins and how quickly it progresses.

  • Size, shape, and density ratios
  • Fill-depth dependence
  • Segregation timescales

Driving Direction

Compare vertical, horizontal, and angled forcing to see how each vibration direction changes the convection pattern.

  • Vertical excitation
  • Horizontal excitation
  • Angled forcing

Environment and Boundaries

Measure how humidity, cohesion, container geometry, and wall friction influence the onset and structure of convection.

  • Humidity and cohesion effects
  • Container geometry
  • Wall-friction dependence

The Mechanism

How vibration turns local grain motion into global circulation.

1

Fluidization

When vibration becomes strong enough, grains briefly lose contact and the bed momentarily fluidizes.

2

Wall Downflow

Friction at the container walls drags grains downward along the boundaries.

3

Center Upflow

Mass conservation forces grains upward in the center, creating two counter-rotating convection rolls.

4

Surface Trapping

Large particles that reach the top cannot easily sink back because smaller grains fill the gap beneath them.
