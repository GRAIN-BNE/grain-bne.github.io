What We Study

Sand can act like a solid, flow like a liquid, and still be neither one.

Granular materials — sand, seeds, glass beads, powders — are collections of macroscopic particles large enough that gravity, not thermal energy, governs their motion. Unlike atoms, grains do not move spontaneously; they require external forcing to rearrange.

That is why granular matter is so strange. It can support weight like a solid, pour like a liquid, dissipate energy in every collision, and suddenly jam mid-flow. Under vibration, local collisions can organize into global convection rolls that transport grains through the entire bed — a phenomenon with no analog in equilibrium thermodynamics and no unified theoretical description.

No Equilibrium

Every collision dissipates energy, so the system must be continually driven to sustain motion.

Force Chains

Stress travels through filamentary contact networks, not uniformly like a fluid.

Brazil Nut Effect

Large particles rise to the surface of a shaken mixture through convection and void-filling dynamics.

Convection Rolls

Wall friction and vibration generate counter-rotating circulation rolls across the entire grain bed.

Research Program

Research Program at Catawba College

GRAIN uses controlled vibration experiments and quantitative imaging to ask how particle properties, forcing parameters, boundary conditions, and ambient environment shape the onset and dynamics of granular convection.

Project I

Segregation Onset

Determine how grain-property ratios and fill depth control when segregation begins and how rapidly it progresses.

  • › Size, shape, and density ratios
  • › Fill-depth dependence
  • › Segregation timescales
  • › Reverse Brazil Nut Effect conditions
Project II

Driving Direction

Compare vertical, horizontal, and angled forcing to characterize how vibration axis changes the convection pattern and segregation efficiency.

  • › Vertical excitation
  • › Horizontal excitation
  • › Angled and compound forcing
  • › Dimensionless Γ parameter space
Project III

Environment & Boundaries

Measure how humidity, cohesion, container geometry, and wall friction influence the onset and structure of convection rolls.

  • › Humidity and cohesion effects
  • › Container geometry
  • › Wall-friction dependence
  • › Pressure and confinement effects
The Mechanism

How vibration turns local grain motion into global circulation.

1

Fluidization

When vibration amplitude is sufficient (Γ = A(2πf)²/g > 1), grains briefly lose contact and the bed momentarily fluidizes.

2

Wall Downflow

Friction at container walls drags grains downward along the boundaries during the compression phase.

3

Center Upflow

Mass conservation forces grains upward through the center, forming two counter-rotating convection rolls.

4

Surface Trapping

Large particles reaching the surface cannot easily sink back — smaller grains fill the void beneath them before they descend.

Methods

Measurement Approach

Quantitative measurements require careful calibration of driving parameters and optical imaging systems. We use Particle Tracking Velocimetry (PTV) and image processing to extract grain-level kinematics from high-speed video.

Particle Tracking Velocimetry

Frame-by-frame tracking of individual grain positions to reconstruct velocity fields and identify convection roll structure.

Vibration Characterization

Accelerometer-based measurement of the dimensionless peak acceleration Γ and frequency f to ensure reproducible driving conditions.

Image Processing Pipeline

Automated segmentation and grain-size classification from high-speed frames; statistical analysis of segregation state over time.

Parameter Space Surveys

Systematic sweeps of frequency, amplitude, size ratio, and fill depth to map phase boundaries of the convection-onset transition.
